Authorizations running out: the quiet write-off

4 min readMay 2026

An authorization is a payer's approval for a specific number of units or hours over a specific period. Once that period ends or those units run out, every additional visit is delivered outside the approval, and outside-approval visits are generally not billable no matter how medically appropriate they were.

0 units authorized units remaining is when billing stops

Authorizations expire on a schedule, not when convenient

Most payer authorizations run for a fixed period, commonly three to twelve months, and require renewal before that period ends to keep billing continuous. Unlike a certification, which is caregiver-specific, an authorization is client-specific, and a large client roster means tracking dozens of independent expiry dates simultaneously.

The write-off often shows up as a surprise months later

A client authorized in January for a six-month period whose renewal is missed in July continues receiving care as scheduled, since the care itself has not changed. The billing problem does not surface until claims for July and August start coming back denied, by which point weeks of visits have already been delivered outside the authorization with no path to bill for them.

Units remaining matters as much as the expiry date

Some authorizations cap total units rather than running purely on a time period, and a client using more units per week than authorized will exhaust the authorization before its expiry date arrives. Tracking authorized units against actual units delivered, not just the calendar date, catches this version of the same problem.

A renewal calendar, checked weekly, closes the gap

The fix mirrors certification tracking: a report of every client's authorization status, including units remaining, reviewed on a fixed weekly cadence with enough lead time to start the renewal process before the authorization actually lapses. Built into the same weekly compliance review as certification tracking, it takes little additional effort once the habit exists.
