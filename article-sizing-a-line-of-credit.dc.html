Sizing a line of credit for a growing agency

6 min readJun 2026

The right size for a line of credit is not a round number picked because it sounds comfortable. It follows directly from how slowly the payer mix collects and how fast the agency is adding clients.

45 days of payroll, a reasonable starting facility size

Start with the payer-weighted collection cycle

An agency's blended days sales outstanding, weighted by how much revenue each payer represents, is the starting point. A blend around 35 to 40 days means roughly that many days of billed revenue are sitting as AR at any given time, financed by cash reserves or the credit facility rather than sitting in the bank.

Growth adds a second layer on top of the lag

A stable agency's AR balance is roughly steady, since new claims replace collected ones at about the same pace. A growing agency's AR balance grows too, since new client volume adds new claims faster than the existing backlog collects. Sizing a facility for a stable-state agency and then growing 20% in client count without revisiting that facility size is a common way agencies find themselves against their limit.

Fixed-asset purchases and the third pay period both need headroom too

A vehicle purchase, an equipment upgrade or a month containing an extra payroll run all draw on the same facility that funds day-to-day AR lag. A limit sized exactly to cover typical AR financing, with no cushion for these predictable but irregular draws, tends to run tight in exactly the months an agency can least afford it.

A reasonable starting point, then revisit annually

Roughly 45 days of fully-loaded payroll is a common starting benchmark for facility size in a payer-lag business, before adjusting for the specific payer mix and growth trajectory. Whatever the starting number, it is worth revisiting at least annually against actual peak utilization, since an agency growing steadily will typically need to raise its limit well before it feels forced to.
