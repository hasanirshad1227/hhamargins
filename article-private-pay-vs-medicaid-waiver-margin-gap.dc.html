Private pay vs Medicaid Waiver: the margin gap explained

5 min readMay 2026

Two clients, the same caregiver role, similar hours a week, and one payer contributes four to five times what the other does to the bottom line. The gap is not staffing. It is the payer.

4-5x typical margin gap, private pay over Medicaid Waiver

The rate difference is smaller than the margin difference

Private pay commonly bills around $34.00 an hour against Medicaid Waiver's $26.50, a difference of about 28%. The margin gap between the two payers is usually far larger than 28%, because rate is only one of three variables that separate them.

Collection speed compounds the rate gap

Private pay typically collects in about twelve days. Medicaid Waiver often takes 45 to 55. That difference means private-pay revenue funds next week's payroll almost immediately, while Medicaid Waiver revenue sits as AR for a month and a half, financed in the meantime by cash reserves or a line of credit that carries its own interest cost.

Denials are the third variable

Private pay clients rarely have claims denied, since there is no third-party payer adjudicating the bill. Medicaid Waiver claims are reviewed against authorization limits, documentation requirements and unit caps, and a share of billed revenue is routinely written off to denials before it ever reaches the bank.

This is not an argument against Medicaid Waiver

Most agencies with any scale need a mixed payer base, and Medicaid Waiver often represents the largest and most stable source of referrals in a market. The point of knowing the gap is not to avoid the lower-margin payer. It is to price staffing decisions, growth plans and cash reserves around the real economics of each payer instead of an average that overstates what Waiver actually contributes and understates what private pay does.
